## What Should You Look for in a Flood Damage Cleanup Company?

A flood cleanup company worth hiring holds an IICRC WRT (Water Damage Restoration Technician) or ASD (Applied Structural Drying) certification, answers calls 24/7, bills your insurance carrier directly, and documents moisture readings room by room before, during, and after drying.

The certification matters because flood water that enters a home from outside, say, overland flow from a swollen creek, is classified as Category 3 water under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/). That classification changes everything about the job: which materials can be dried in place versus removed, what PPE the crew wears, and what antimicrobial treatment the drying plan requires. A company without a [certified technician](https://www.iicrc.org/) on site is guessing at a process that has an actual standard behind it.

Moisture mapping documentation isn't paperwork for its own sake. It's the evidence your insurance adjuster needs to approve the claim, and it's the proof that the structure was actually dry before the drywall went back up. Ask any company you're considering to show you a sample moisture log before you sign anything.

## How Fast Should a Flood Cleanup Company Respond in Rockville?

Response time matters most in the first 24 to 48 hours, the window in which wet drywall, subflooring, and framing can begin growing mold according to the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). Waiting two or three days for a callback can turn a drying job into a materials-removal job.

This is especially true for the older split-level and rambler basements common around Rockville, Kensington, and parts of Silver Spring. Many of these homes sit close to Rock Creek or Watts Branch tributaries, where flash flooding after a heavy summer storm can push water into a finished basement in under an hour. Paneling, carpet pad, and particleboard shelving in these basements wick water quickly and hold it even after the visible puddle is gone.

Ask directly: how many technicians can you have on site today, and what equipment are you bringing? A vague answer ("we'll get someone out this week") is itself useful information. ## What Red Flags Should Make You Walk Away?

A few patterns show up again and again with companies that cause more damage than they fix:

- **No certification they can name or show you.** If they can't tell you who on the crew holds an IICRC credential, that's a gap, not a technicality.
- **Demanding full payment up front, cash only.** A legitimate company that regularly works with insurance will walk you through the claim process, not ask you to bypass it.
- **A verbal estimate with no written scope.** Flood damage pricing depends on square footage, category of water, and materials affected. A real estimate reflects that.
- **No moisture documentation plan.** If they can't describe how they'll prove the structure is dry, they can't prove it to your insurer either.
- **Pressure to sign before anyone inspects the damage.** A rushed signature protects the contractor, not you.
