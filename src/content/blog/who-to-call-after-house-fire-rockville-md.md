## Who Should You Call First After a House Fire?

Call 911 first, always, if the fire is still active or anyone is hurt or unaccounted for. Montgomery County Fire and Rescue has to physically walk the structure and declare it safe before anyone, including you, goes back inside, even to grab medication or a phone charger.

After the fire department clears you, the order generally runs: utilities (if the fire marshal flags electrical or gas damage), your insurance company, then a fire damage restoration company to secure the property and start mitigation. If the house isn't livable, ask the responding crew or the Red Cross chapter on scene about emergency lodging before you leave.

Skipping a step costs you later. Entering before clearance can trigger a code violation or void parts of your claim. Calling a restoration company before your insurer knows about the loss can create confusion over who authorized what work.

## What Do You Do in the First Hour After the Fire Is Out?

In the first hour, your job is to secure the scene and start documenting, not to start cleaning. Walk the exterior (not the interior, until cleared) and photograph visible damage: the roofline, windows, siding, anything charred or broken.

Don't run the HVAC system. A furnace or AC unit will pull soot through the ductwork and spread it to rooms the fire never touched, which turns a one-room cleanup into a whole-house one. Leave the system off until a technician has checked the ducts.

Don't throw out damaged items yet, even the obviously ruined ones. Your insurance adjuster and your restoration company both need to see the loss before anything leaves the property. If a window or exterior wall is open to the weather, ask whether the fire department can rope it off or tarp it; if not, that's an emergency board-up call.

The [American Red Cross guide to home fire recovery](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/fire/home-fire-recovery.html) is a good first-hour checklist if you want a second source beyond this one.

## When Do You Call Pepco or Washington Gas?

You call the utility company when the fire marshal flags damage to the electrical panel, wiring or a gas line, which is common even in fires that started somewhere else in the house. Fire crews often red-tag a damaged meter or shut off gas at the street as a safety step, and that service has to be restored by the utility, not by you or a contractor.

If the fire department doesn't mention utility status before they leave, ask directly: "Is the power or gas still connected, and did you notify Pepco or Washington Gas?" Don't attempt to reset a breaker or relight a pilot light yourself after a structure fire. A licensed electrician or the utility has to sign off first.

## What Do You Tell Your Insurance Company First?

Tell your insurer the date, the general cause if known, and whether the home is currently livable, then ask directly about Additional Living Expenses (ALE) coverage if you can't stay in the house. Most homeowners policies cover sudden fire loss, but the specifics (ALE limits, contents coverage, smoke damage to unaffected rooms) vary by policy.

Get a claim number on that first call and ask what documentation they need before cleanup starts. The [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/default.aspx) has consumer resources if a claim gets complicated or you want to understand your rights as a policyholder before you sign anything.

Most adjusters want photos of the damage before any debris is removed or any item is cleaned, so hold off on deep cleanup until you've either had the adjuster out or gotten clear instruction to proceed. A restoration company experienced with insurance work, like the one you already called, can document the loss in a way adjusters expect to see.
