## What Drives the Price of a Mold Remediation Job?

The final number comes down to how much contaminated material has to be physically removed and how hard the space is to access, not just the square footage of visible mold. Six factors move the price the most.

1. **Extent and class of contamination.** A few spots on drywall is a different job than mold that's traveled into insulation, subfloor, or wall framing.
2. **Porous vs. non-porous materials.** Drywall, carpet, and insulation usually have to be removed and replaced. Tile, concrete, and metal can often be cleaned and treated in place.
3. **Accessibility.** Crawl spaces and tight attics take longer per square foot because of the physical constraints on containment and equipment setup.
4. **Containment complexity.** A one-room job needs basic poly sheeting; a whole-house job needs multiple containment zones and negative air pressure to keep spores from spreading during work.
5. **Whether the moisture source is included.** Mold always comes back if the leak, grading issue, or humidity problem that caused it isn't fixed. Jobs that bundle in [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) or plumbing repair cost more up front but avoid a repeat job.
6. **Clearance testing.** Post-remediation air or surface sampling by a third party adds a few hundred dollars but gives documented proof the space is clear, which matters for resale and insurance.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Pay for Mold Remediation in Maryland?

Maryland homeowners policies generally cover mold remediation only when the mold results from a sudden, accidental, covered event, like a burst supply line or a storm-driven roof leak, not from a slow plumbing leak, high humidity, or deferred maintenance. Most standard policies cap mold-specific coverage even when it is covered, often between $1,000 and $10,000, and insurers typically expect the underlying water source to be repaired promptly.

The practical difference is documentation. An adjuster wants to see when the water event happened, what caused it, and that remediation started quickly, according to general guidance from the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). A detailed moisture map, photos, and a written scope of work from day one make the difference between a paid claim and a denied one. If a claim gets disputed, the [Maryland Insurance Administration](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/default.aspx) is the state resource for filing a complaint or checking an insurer's obligations.

## What Happens During a Mold Remediation Job?

A remediation job follows a fixed sequence regardless of size, built around the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for professional mold remediation.

1. **Inspection and moisture mapping.** A technician identifies the source, measures moisture content in surrounding materials, and documents the extent of growth.
2. **Containment setup.** Poly sheeting and negative air machines isolate the work area so spores don't spread to clean parts of the house during demolition.
3. **HEPA air scrubbing.** Air scrubbers run continuously to capture airborne spores while material removal is underway.
4. **Removal of contaminated material.** Porous materials that can't be cleaned, like soaked drywall or carpet padding, come out and go into sealed bags before leaving the containment zone.
5. **Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.** Remaining structural surfaces get HEPA vacuumed and treated per [IICRC S520 protocol](https://iicrc.org/s520/).
6. **Drying.** Dehumidifiers and air movers bring moisture content back to normal before any rebuild starts.
7. **Clearance testing.** A final check confirms spore counts are back to a normal background level.
8. **Rebuild.** Drywall, flooring, and trim go back in once the space passes clearance.
