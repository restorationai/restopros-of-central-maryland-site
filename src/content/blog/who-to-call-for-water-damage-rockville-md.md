## Who Should I Call First for Water Damage in Rockville?

Call a licensed water damage restoration company first, not your insurance company and not a general contractor. [RestoPros of Central Maryland](/) answers around the clock at [(240) 261-1639](tel:+12402611639), holds IICRC WRT (Water Restoration Technician) certification, and is licensed and insured for water damage work across Rockville and Montgomery County.

A restoration company's job is to stop the damage from getting worse: extracting standing water, pulling wet baseboards and flooring where needed, and setting up commercial dehumidifiers and air movers to dry the structure according to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). That's a different skill set than fixing the pipe or appliance that caused the leak, which is why the call order matters.

If you're not sure yet how bad it is, our [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) page walks through what a typical assessment covers, and the [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) has the first five minutes laid out step by step.

## Should I Call a Plumber or a Restoration Company First?

Call a plumber first only if water is still actively flowing from a pipe, valve, or fixture you can't shut off yourself. If the water source is already stopped (you closed the main shutoff, the washing machine finished its cycle and the hose split, the dishwasher is unplugged), call the restoration company next: the water on your floors and in your walls won't stop damaging the structure just because the leak itself is fixed.

In practice, many Rockville homes need both calls within the same hour. If you can't locate your main shutoff valve or the leak is coming from inside a wall, a [plumber or restoration technician familiar with burst pipe repair](/services/burst-pipe-repair/) can often handle the shutoff as part of the first visit. If there's any exposed wiring, a breaker tripping near water, or a gas smell, call 911 or your utility company before anyone touches the area.

## What Happens When You Call a Water Damage Company?

The first call is mostly triage: the dispatcher asks what's wet, how long it's been wet, and whether the water source is a clean supply line, an appliance, or something like a sewer backup. Have ready the approximate size of the affected area, whether it's a basement, main floor, or upper floor, and whether you've already shut off the water.

On arrival, a technician inspects with a moisture meter and, often, a thermal camera to find water that's traveled into walls or under flooring, not just what's visible on the surface. From there they set up extraction equipment for any standing water, then place air movers and a dehumidifier sized to the room. Photos and moisture readings get logged at this stage too. This documentation is what an adjuster will later want to see, which is why starting it on day one (not after you've already dried things out yourself) protects your claim.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after mitigation has started, not before, since your policy's duty-to-mitigate clause means prompt action protects the claim either way. Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed appliance hose, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and long-term seepage, and they usually require a separate sewer/sump backup endorsement for drain backups or sump pump failures.

Document the damage with photos and keep any receipts for emergency services before you call.
