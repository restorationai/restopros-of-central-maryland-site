## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Rockville, MD?

Most Rockville-area water damage jobs fall between $1,200 and $10,000, with sewage backups and multi-level losses running higher. The table below reflects typical ranges for common scenarios across Montgomery, Prince George's, and Howard County homes.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|

| Burst pipe affecting 2-3 rooms (Category 1-2) | $2,800 - $6,500 |

These ranges cover extraction, structural drying, and sanitizing. They do not include replacing flooring, cabinetry, or drywall once drying is complete, since that work is typically priced separately once the scope is known.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down?

The single biggest factor is the water's category under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), which determines how much material has to be removed versus dried in place. A failed supply line (Category 1, clean water) can often be dried without tearing out drywall. A sewer backup (Category 3, black water) requires removing porous materials like carpet, pad, and baseboards, plus antimicrobial treatment, which adds labor and disposal cost.

Square footage and how long the water sat before anyone called also matter. A leak caught within hours affects less material than one that soaked a finished basement overnight. Materials matter too: solid hardwood (Class 4 drying) takes specialized drying mats and longer timelines than carpet over concrete. Response speed is the one factor a homeowner can actually control, since water that sits for 24 to 48 hours gives mold a real head start, per the [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home). If mold has already taken hold by the time drying starts, that adds a separate mold remediation scope to the job.

A recent job we documented, a [hidden pipe leak behind a kitchen wall](/case-studies/hidden-pipe-leak-behind-a-kitchen-wall/), shows how a loss that looks small on the surface can involve more material removal than expected once the wall is opened.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover the Cost?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a pipe that bursts or an appliance hose that suddenly fails, but they exclude gradual leaks, long-term seepage, and ordinary wear and tear. That distinction is often what separates a paid claim from a denied one.

Sewer and drain backups, along with sump pump failure, are typically NOT covered unless the policy has a separate water backup endorsement added. If your basement flooded because a sump pump failed, check your policy for that specific rider before assuming it's covered. Our full breakdown on [what homeowners insurance actually covers](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walks through the documentation adjusters expect.

Homeowners also have a duty to mitigate damage once a loss happens, meaning stopping the water and starting drying promptly protects the claim rather than hurting it. Photograph everything before cleanup begins, and keep every receipt. Our crews provide the moisture readings, drying logs, and photo documentation adjusters ask for, which is often what moves a claim from questioned to approved.

## What Happens During a Water Damage Restoration Job?

A typical job moves through four phases: inspection and water extraction, structural drying, cleaning and sanitizing, and a final moisture check before closeout. Here's what each step actually involves.

1. **Inspection and extraction.** A technician identifies the water's source and category, then removes standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment.
2. **Structural drying.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed to dry affected materials in place where possible, following IICRC S500 drying class guidelines. Moisture meters track progress daily, not just by eye.
3. **Cleaning and sanitizing.** Category 2 and 3 water requires antimicrobial treatment of affected surfaces to address contamination risk, not just moisture.
4. **Final verification.** Moisture readings confirm materials are back to a dry standard before the job closes, which also protects against hidden mold growth later.
