## What Does Professional Sewage Cleanup Actually Involve?

Professional sewage cleanup means extracting contaminated water, removing anything porous that absorbed it, disinfecting the structure, and verifying the air and surfaces are safe before rebuilding. It's a sequence, not a single visit.

The process typically starts with extraction using truck-mounted or portable pumps, followed by removal of affected carpet, carpet pad, drywall below the waterline, and insulation, since these materials can't be fully disinfected. Technicians then apply EPA-registered antimicrobial treatments to studs, subfloor, and remaining structure, and set up negative air pressure with HEPA air scrubbers to control airborne contaminants during the work. Drying follows the [IICRC S500](https://www.iicrc.org) standard, with daily moisture monitoring until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just until it looks dry.

In older Rockville neighborhoods like Twinbrook and the West End, where many homes still run on clay or cast-iron sewer laterals installed before the 1970s, root intrusion and pipe collapse are the leading causes of the backups we see. Newer construction near King Farm and Fallsgrove tends to have fewer lateral failures but is not immune to sump pump failure during heavy rain.

## How Dangerous Is Raw Sewage in a Home?

Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 water, also called black water, the most contaminated tier in the IICRC's water damage classification system. It can carry E. coli, salmonella, hepatitis A, and parasitic organisms.

That classification isn't academic. It determines what gets thrown away versus dried. Under IICRC guidelines, porous materials like carpet, upholstery, and drywall that contact Category 3 water are generally considered unsalvageable because disinfectant can't reach contamination trapped in the fiber or paper backing. Hard surfaces like tile, sealed concrete, and solid wood can often be cleaned and disinfected in place. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov) notes that sewage-contaminated water should be treated as a biohazard, with direct contact avoided until the area is professionally treated. Anyone with a compromised immune system, young children, or pets should stay out of the affected rooms entirely until cleanup is complete.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Sewage Backup?

Most standard homeowners policies exclude sewer and drain backups unless you've added a water backup endorsement, a separate rider many Maryland homeowners don't realize they're missing until they need it.

Without that endorsement, a backup caused by a municipal sewer line failure or your own clogged lateral is typically not covered. With it, coverage usually extends to cleanup, disinfection, and drying costs, sometimes with a lower sublimit than the policy's main dwelling coverage. The claim depends heavily on documentation: photos of standing water before cleanup starts, a written moisture log, and an itemized list of removed materials. ## What Should You Do Right After a Sewage Backup?

Stop using any water fixtures in the home immediately, then keep everyone, including pets, away from the affected area until a professional arrives.

A few concrete steps protect both your health and your claim:

1. **Shut off water use** to any fixture connected to the blocked line, this prevents the backup from getting worse.
2. **Don't attempt DIY cleanup** on Category 3 water with household cleaners, they aren't rated for this level of contamination.
3. **Photograph the damage** before anything is moved or removed, from multiple angles and with a visible timestamp if your phone supports it.
4. **Call a certified restoration crew**, not a general handyman, sewage work requires specific PPE, containment, and disposal protocols.
5. **Contact your insurer after mitigation has started**, not before, since most policies require you to prevent further damage first.
