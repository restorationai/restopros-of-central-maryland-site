## What makes sewage cleanup different from regular water damage cleanup?

Sewage backup is always treated as Category 3 water, also called black water, because it's grossly contaminated regardless of how it entered your home. That means a different cleanup standard applies than for a clean supply-line leak.

Under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), Category 3 water requires removal of porous materials that can't be fully disinfected, like carpet pad, drywall below the waterline, and insulation, rather than just drying them in place. Technicians also need antimicrobial treatment of the affected area and personal protective equipment, including gloves, rubber boots, and respiratory protection, because of the bacteria and pathogens involved. A company that tries to dry your carpet instead of removing it after a sewage backup isn't following the standard, and it's worth asking directly how they classify the water and what their removal plan is before they start.

Many older homes in Rockville and the surrounding Montgomery County neighborhoods still run on original cast-iron or clay sewer laterals, which are more prone to the root intrusion and pipe breaks that cause backups in the first place. That doesn't change the cleanup protocol, but it's worth mentioning to whoever you call so they know what they're likely dealing with underground.

## Does homeowners insurance cover sewage backup cleanup?

Usually not by default. Most standard homeowners policies exclude sewer and drain backups unless you've added a water backup, sometimes called sewer and sump, endorsement to the policy.

According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance), sudden and accidental water damage from inside the home is typically covered, but backups from sewers, drains, or sump pump failures need that specific endorsement, which many homeowners don't realize they're missing until after a loss. Check your declarations page for a water backup rider before you assume coverage either way. If you do have that endorsement, document everything: photos before cleanup starts, a written scope of work, and an itemized list of what's being removed. A cleanup company that provides clear documentation makes that claim conversation with your adjuster far easier than one that hands you a vague invoice afterward.
