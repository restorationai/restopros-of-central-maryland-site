## What should a storm damage company be able to do on day one?

A storm damage company should be able to stop the damage from getting worse the same day you call, not just schedule an estimate. That means emergency tarping over a compromised roof, board-up for broken windows or doors, and water extraction equipment already on the truck.

Storm damage rarely stays contained to the exterior. Wind-driven rain gets behind siding, through roof penetrations, and into attic insulation within hours. A company that only offers an inspection visit, with the actual repair crew scheduled for "next week," is not equipped to handle an active loss. ## What certifications and licensing should you verify?

Ask for proof of general liability insurance and confirm the technicians have formal water damage training, not just "years of experience." IICRC's Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) credential, built on the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), is the industry benchmark for how a wet structure should actually be dried.

A company that can't produce proof of insurance, or gets vague when you ask who is certified and in what, is a red flag. RestoPros of Central Maryland's technicians carry IICRC WRT training, and the company is licensed and insured, information any legitimate company should hand over without hesitation.

## How should the company handle your insurance claim?

A good storm damage company documents the loss with photos, moisture readings, and a written scope before work starts, and it's willing to communicate directly with your adjuster. That documentation is often the difference between a claim that gets paid promptly and one that gets disputed.

Most homeowners policies cover sudden storm damage, wind, hail, and a tree falling on the roof, but flood water from rising groundwater or overflowing streams typically needs a separate flood policy, per the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). Maryland homeowners with coverage questions can also check the [Maryland Insurance Administration's consumer resources](https://insurance.maryland.gov/Consumer/Pages/default.aspx). A company that photographs the damage, logs moisture readings room by room, and gives you a written scope of work is protecting your claim, not just its own invoice. If you're still unsure what a reputable estimate should look like, our [guide to choosing a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/) walks through the red flags in more detail.

## What should you ask before you sign anything?

Before signing a work order, ask these five questions: Who answers the phone after hours? Is the crew IICRC trained, and in what? What's included in the written scope, and what's billed separately? Will you document the loss for my insurance adjuster? And can you start mitigation today, not next week?

A company that answers all five clearly, in writing, is one you can trust with a storm claim. One that dodges two or three of them is worth a second call to someone else.
