## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Bethesda

Much of Bethesda's single-family housing dates to the mid-20th century, brick and frame colonials and ramblers with basements rather than slab foundations, a layout that routes water damage straight to framing, insulation, and finished lower-level living space. Homes from that era commonly carry galvanized or early copper supply lines and may include asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe wrap, or old plaster, so mitigation scopes have to account for testing before demolition in those sections. Newer mid-rise and high-rise construction closer to the commercial district introduces different mechanical and plumbing systems, with shared risers and HVAC that carry a leak's effects well beyond the unit of origin.

The area sits on Piedmont terrain with clay-heavy soils that hold water against foundations and slow drainage after heavy rain, a factor in the basement seepage and crawlspace moisture we're regularly asked to address.
