## Why Kensington Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of the housing stock in and around Kensington predates modern plumbing codes, which means galvanized supply lines and cast iron drain stacks are still doing work they were never designed to do for this long. When those fail, they often fail slowly, a pinhole leak behind a wall or under a slab that goes unnoticed until flooring starts to cup or a ceiling stain appears. Add in the humid Mid-Atlantic summers common to this part of Maryland, and any hidden moisture has a head start on mold growth, sometimes within 24 to 48 hours of the original leak. Heavy regional thunderstorms and the occasional nor'easter also push water through grading and window wells on older lots where the soil has settled over decades, which is a different failure mode than a straightforward supply line burst.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Kensington

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much access the property allows. From there we move to moisture mapping, checking subfloor, framing, and wall cavities with meters rather than guessing based on what's visible at the surface, because in a lot of Kensington's older homes the surface dries faster than the structure underneath. Drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, gets set per IICRC S500 guidelines, and we track moisture content daily until materials return to a dry standard rather than just "looks dry." For homes with crawl spaces, which are common on the older residential streets here, we pay particular attention to vapor barriers and sill plate moisture, since that's where we most often find lingering saturation after the visible water is gone.
