## Why Hyattsville Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Hyattsville's housing was built in the early-to-mid twentieth century, which means plumbing systems are frequently a mix of original cast iron, later galvanized repairs, and in some cases newer PEX patched in during a remodel. Add to that Prince George's County's clay-heavy soil, which drains slowly and pushes groundwater toward foundations during sustained rain, and basements in this area take on water even when the roof and gutters are in good shape. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Hyattsville

The first visit is about stopping the loss, not just documenting it. We extract standing water with truck-mounted and portable units, then move to moisture mapping, because surface-dry drywall or subfloor in these older homes frequently hides saturation in the framing or subfloor beneath. Structural drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed according to the moisture readings rather than a fixed formula, since a 1940s bungalow with plaster walls dries at a different rate than a newer addition with drywall. We recheck moisture daily and don't close out drying until readings confirm the structure is back to a dry standard, not just visually dry.
