## Why Edgemere Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Edgemere's housing stock started as seasonal cottage construction along the shoreline communities of the North Point peninsula, later converted into year-round homes. That history shows up in crawlspace foundations that sit closer to grade than you'd find further inland, slab additions tacked onto original framing, and plumbing runs that were never designed for modern washer and dishwasher loads. Add a high water table so close to the Patapsco, and you get sump pumps working overtime during heavy rain, groundwater pressure pushing moisture through foundation walls, and crawlspaces that stay damp long after the storm passes. Many properties here are also on well and septic rather than county water and sewer, which changes how we approach any loss involving gray or black water and whether local health code triggers apply.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Edgemere

The first call is always triage: is the water clean, gray, or contaminated, and is it still rising. From there our crew extracts standing water with truck-mount or portable extraction units depending on access, because narrow waterfront lots and gravel driveways common in this part of Baltimore County don't always accommodate a full-size rig close to the door. Once the bulk water is gone, we pull baseboards, check wall cavities with a moisture meter, and set containment zones around crawlspaces, which in Edgemere often means working in tighter, lower-clearance spaces than a typical slab-on-grade suburban build. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the actual cubic footage and material type, not a flat formula. We re-check moisture content daily until subfloor, framing, and insulation read dry, not just the surface.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of out-of-area contractors: Edgemere's older cottage-converted homes frequently have crawlspace vents that were sealed or insulated over during a past renovation, which traps moisture instead of letting it escape. When we're assessing a crawlspace water loss here, we check for blocked or painted-over vents before we commit to a drying plan, because a sealed crawlspace dries on a completely different timeline than a vented one, and skipping that check means you can declare a space dry when it isn't.
