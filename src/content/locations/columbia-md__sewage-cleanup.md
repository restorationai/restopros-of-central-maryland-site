## Why Columbia Sees Sewage Backups

Columbia was built out in planned phases starting in the 1960s, which means a lot of the underground sewer infrastructure serving the original villages is now 50 to 60 years old. Clay and early-generation cast iron lines from that era are more prone to root intrusion and joint failure than modern PVC, and Howard County's clay-heavy Piedmont soil holds moisture that accelerates pipe corrosion and ground shifting around aging laterals. Add in Maryland's summer thunderstorm pattern, when several inches of rain can fall in under an hour, and the county's combined stormwater and sanitary infrastructure in older sections can surcharge, pushing sewage back up through floor drains and basement fixtures in homes that have never had a backup before. Townhome clusters and condo buildings common throughout Columbia's village layout also share sewer laterals in some cases, so a clog or collapse two doors down can show up as a backup in a unit that did nothing wrong.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

We start by identifying the contamination category, since raw sewage is classified as Category 3 "black water" and is treated differently from clean or gray water losses. Standing sewage and saturated porous materials, carpet pad, baseboards, drywall below the flood line, are removed rather than dried, because porous materials that have contacted raw sewage can't be reliably sanitized. Hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor get cleaned, treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and dried using the same moisture-mapping approach we use on water jobs, following IICRC S500 drying benchmarks. Air scrubbers and negative air setups keep aerosolized contaminants from spreading to unaffected rooms while work is underway, which matters more in Columbia's many attached and semi-attached homes where airflow between units can be a real concern.

## Columbia Insurance & HOA Coordination

Sewer backup coverage is often a separate rider from standard homeowners policies, so we document the source of the backup, affected materials, and disposal of contaminated contents in detail to support that specific claim line. In Columbia's HOA-governed communities, which cover a large share of the city's townhomes and condos, shared-line backups frequently involve a question of whether the HOA's master policy or the individual owner's policy responds first. We photograph and log everything as we go so that detail doesn't become a dispute later, and we can speak directly with property managers or board representatives when a shared lateral is involved.

## Local note

A good share of Columbia's original village centers, places like Wilde Lake and Oakland Mills in their earlier phases, were built with sanitary laterals that tie into trunk lines sized for 1960s-era household usage. Decades of added density and impervious surface runoff mean those same lines now carry more volume during peak storm events than they were designed for, which is part of why backups in older sections tend to cluster during or right after heavy rain rather than showing up on dry days.
