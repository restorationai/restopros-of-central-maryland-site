## Why Hyattsville Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Hyattsville's housing stock spans early-1900s bungalows near the Route 1 corridor to mid-century brick homes and newer infill construction, and each era handles wind and water differently. Older roofs with original flashing details are more likely to let wind-driven rain past the shingle line during a hard-driving storm, while homes closer to the floodplain areas along the Anacostia tributaries can take on water through foundation walls even when the roof holds fine. Prince George's County's clay-heavy soil also drains slowly, so yards and window wells that pond after a downpour tend to stay saturated for days, which matters when we're assessing how far moisture has traveled into a structure. Add in the tree cover, and wind events regularly bring down limbs onto roofs, decks, and fence lines well after the storm itself has passed through.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hyattsville

We start with a structural safety check, because a storm-damaged roof or a leaning tree limb can hide hazards that aren't obvious from the ground. From there we tarp or board open areas to stop ongoing intrusion, extract any standing water, and document everything with photos before we move anything, since that documentation is what your adjuster will want to see. Materials get tested for moisture content, not just visual dryness, because a damp subfloor or soaked wall cavity can look fine on the surface while staying wet underneath. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, with monitoring continued until readings confirm the structure is actually dry, not just dry enough to look done.
