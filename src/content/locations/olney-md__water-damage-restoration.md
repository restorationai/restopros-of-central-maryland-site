## Why Olney Properties See Water Damage Issues

Olney sits in upper Montgomery County on the kind of clay-heavy Piedmont soil that drains slowly after a hard rain. When summer thunderstorms dump an inch or two in under an hour, that clay holds water against foundations longer than sandier soil would, and basements with even minor grading issues take on seepage hours after the sky clears. A lot of the housing stock here dates to the suburban growth Olney saw from the 1960s through the 1980s, split-levels and colonials with finished basements that were never built with a modern vapor barrier. Add in the older homes still running on private well and septic systems rather than county water and sewer, and a failed well pressure tank or a septic backup can introduce water damage and contamination in the same event, which changes how we approach extraction and drying.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Olney

The first thing we do on arrival is identify the water category, clean supply line, groundwater, or something that's picked up contaminants from a septic or sewer backup, because that determines whether we're doing straight extraction or a mitigation job with disposal requirements. From there it's moisture mapping with a meter and thermal imaging to find water that's migrated into wall cavities or under flooring, high-capacity extraction to pull standing water fast, and then placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage. Drywall and subfloor in Olney's older split-levels often hides water longer than it should, especially where a finished basement ceiling traps moisture against the joists above, so we recheck readings over several days rather than pulling equipment the moment surfaces feel dry. Everything is logged against the IICRC S500 drying standard so there's a clear record for your insurance carrier.

## Olney Insurance & HOA Coordination

A fair number of Olney's newer communities are HOA-governed, and some of those associations have their own notification requirements or preferred vendor lists for water losses that touch shared walls, townhome party walls, or common-area plumbing. We document the source, the affected materials, and the drying progress with photos and moisture logs so that paperwork is ready whether it's going to your homeowner's policy, an HOA claim, or both. For well and septic properties, we also note whether the water source was potable or needed to be treated as contaminated, since that affects what a carrier will approve for material removal.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Olney homeowners: a slow basement seep after a storm often isn't the storm itself, it's the clay soil around the foundation releasing water for a day or two afterward. We've learned to keep moisture meters running on affected framing for longer than you'd expect in sandier parts of the county, because a basement that reads dry 24 hours post-storm can still be feeding moisture up from a saturated footing below.
