## Why Gaithersburg Properties See Mold Issues

Gaithersburg sits in the humid subtropical belt that covers most of central Maryland, which means long stretches of 80-90% outdoor humidity from June through September. Homes built with central air from that era weren't always designed with vapor barriers or modern ventilation in mind, so basements and crawlspaces trap moisture that condenses on cool foundation walls. Add a finished rec room or a converted basement office, which is common in this part of Montgomery County, and you have drywall, carpet, and insulation sitting against damp concrete for months at a time. Once a roof leak, sump pump failure, or even a slow HVAC condensate drip adds standing moisture to that mix, colonization can start within 24 to 48 hours.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Gaithersburg

We start every job with a moisture map, using thermal imaging and a hygrometer to find where water is actually traveling, not just where it's visible. In older Gaithersburg homes with plaster or real wood lath, we often find mold has migrated further from the original source than the stain on the surface suggests, so containment gets built larger than the visibly affected area. Negative air machines and physical poly barriers isolate the work zone from the rest of the house before anything is cut out or removed. Affected materials, whether that's drywall, baseboard, or subfloor, are removed and bagged, surfaces are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and the space is dried down using IICRC-informed moisture control protocols before we close out the job.

## Montgomery County Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Gaithersburg homeowners' policies treat mold caused by a sudden, covered event, like a burst supply line, differently than mold from long-term humidity or a known deferred leak, so documenting the source matters as much as documenting the damage. We photograph and log moisture readings throughout the job to support a claim either way. For properties inside an HOA or condo association, which is common in several of Gaithersburg's newer developments, we're used to providing the kind of written scope and completion documentation a board or management company asks for before they'll sign off on interior work.

## Local note

Across Montgomery County, the clay-heavy Piedmont soil underneath most neighborhoods holds water longer than sandier soil further east, which is part of why sump pumps are standard equipment in so many local basements rather than an upgrade. When a sump pump fails or a discharge line gets clogged with leaf debris in the fall, that water backs up against the foundation and finds its way in through the lowest point, usually a crack or the cove joint where the slab meets the wall. It's a quiet failure that can run for days before a homeowner notices a musty smell or discolored baseboard, which is exactly the kind of slow-building case that turns into a larger mold job than the original leak would suggest.
