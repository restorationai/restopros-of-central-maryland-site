## Why Glen Burnie Homes See Persistent Mold Issues

Glen Burnie sits close enough to the Patapsco and the Chesapeake watershed that summer humidity stays high for months at a time, and a lot of the housing stock here, especially the post-war ranch and split-level homes built through the 1950s and 60s boom, was framed before vapor barriers and crawl space encapsulation were standard practice. Add in slab-on-grade additions and older HVAC systems that struggle to pull moisture out of the air in July and August, and you get the conditions mold needs: surface moisture, organic material (wood framing, paper-faced drywall), and still air. Homes near BWI Marshall Airport and the industrial corridor along Route 2 also tend to have additions and garages converted decades ago without proper ventilation, which is where we most often find colonization that homeowners didn't know was there.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Glen Burnie

Every job starts with identifying the moisture source, because treating mold without fixing what's feeding it just buys you a repeat visit in six months. We isolate the affected area with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't travel into HVAC returns or adjoining rooms, a real concern in the open floor plans common in Glen Burnie's newer townhome developments. From there it's controlled removal of porous materials that can't be salvaged, HEPA vacuuming and antimicrobial treatment of the structure, and a final air exchange to clear the containment zone. We work from the IICRC S500 and S520 standards for drying and mold remediation, which govern how containment, negative air, and clearance are handled on a job like this regardless of the property type.
