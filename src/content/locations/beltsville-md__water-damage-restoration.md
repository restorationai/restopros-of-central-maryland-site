## Why Beltsville Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Beltsville's housing dates to the 1950s and 60s, split-levels and brick ranchers built when plumbing codes allowed galvanized supply lines that are now well past their service life. Add in Prince George's County's dense clay subsoil, which drains slowly even after the rain stops, and you get a pattern we see constantly: sump pumps running for hours after a storm, foundation walls weeping long after the sky clears, and crawlspaces that stay damp for days. Summer in the Mid-Atlantic brings sudden, heavy thunderstorms that overwhelm older storm drains faster than steady winter rain ever does, which is why late June through August tends to be Beltsville's busiest season for basement and crawlspace intrusion calls.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Beltsville

We start by identifying whether you're dealing with clean supply-line water, gray water from an appliance failure, or storm water that picked up contaminants on its way into the structure, because that classification drives everything downstream, including how we document the loss for your carrier. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on access, since a lot of Beltsville's older homes have narrow basement stairwells or crawlspace hatches that rule out full-size equipment. From there we set containment and begin structural drying per IICRC S500 guidelines, monitoring moisture in framing, subfloor, and drywall daily rather than guessing at a fixed timeline. Homes with original plaster or dense older lumber hold moisture longer than new-construction drywall and dimensional lumber, so we adjust drying equipment placement and runtime rather than pulling gear on a generic schedule.

We check subfloor moisture at the perimeter walls first on these homes, not just the visible wet spots, because that's usually where the real saturation is hiding.
