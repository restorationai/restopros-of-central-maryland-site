## Why Gaithersburg Properties See Water Damage Issues

Montgomery County sits on clay-heavy soil typical of the Piedmont region, which drains slowly after heavy rain. That means foundations and basement walls in Gaithersburg stay under hydrostatic pressure longer after a storm than they would in sandier soil, and it's a common reason sump pumps that have run fine for years suddenly can't keep up during a multi-day system. Add in aging supply lines in homes from the 60s through 80s, and the two most common calls we get are failed sump pumps during heavy rain and slow pinhole leaks from galvanized or early copper plumbing behind walls and under slab.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Gaithersburg

The first step on any call is stopping the source and extracting standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, because every hour water sits on flooring or subfloor is an hour closer to warping, delamination, or mold colonization, which can begin in as little as 24 to 48 hours in a humid Maryland summer. From there we map moisture with thermal imaging and penetrating meters to find out how far water has traveled under flooring and inside wall cavities, not just where it's visible. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings rather than a fixed schedule. For finished basements, which are common throughout Gaithersburg, that often means pulling baseboards and drilling small weep holes in drywall to dry the wall cavity instead of tearing out material that doesn't need to go.
