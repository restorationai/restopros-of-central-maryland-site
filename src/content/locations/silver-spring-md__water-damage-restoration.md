## Why Silver Spring Properties See Water Damage Issues

Silver Spring's housing stock spans a wide range, from brick colonials and Cape Cods built in the postwar decades to newer mid-rise and high-rise condo buildings near the downtown core. The older homes often have cast iron or galvanized supply lines nearing the end of their service life, and basements that were finished decades after the original build without a proper vapor barrier. Water finds those gaps fast. In the taller residential buildings, a failed washing machine supply hose or a unit-to-unit leak can travel through multiple floors before anyone notices, soaking ceilings and walls well below the source. Montgomery County's clay-heavy soil also holds water against foundation walls longer after heavy rain, which keeps hydrostatic pressure on older basement walls and makes seasonal seepage a recurring issue for homes near low-lying stretches close to Sligo Creek and the Northwest Branch.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Silver Spring

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find how far water has traveled behind drywall, under flooring, and into framing, because a floor that looks dry on the surface can still be saturated underneath. In homes with plaster-and-lath walls, common in Silver Spring's older neighborhoods, we adjust drying time upward since plaster holds and releases moisture more slowly than drywall, even though extraction from the surface is usually quick. For condo and apartment units, we coordinate with property management early, since shared walls and HVAC chases mean a leak on one floor can affect the ceiling below, and building engineers often need to shut off risers before we can safely extract. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage, with equipment placement and air movement calibrated to the specific materials in each structure rather than a one-size approach.

## Local Note

Montgomery County's water and sewer system is run by WSSC Water rather than a city utility, which matters during heavy storm events: older sewer lines in parts of Silver Spring can surcharge under heavy rainfall, pushing water back up through basement drains and floor fixtures. When we see this pattern, we flag it separately from a plumbing failure, because the mitigation approach and the insurance conversation both change when the water source is a municipal backup rather than an internal pipe.
