## Why Garrison Properties See Water Damage Issues

Garrison's housing stock skews older in parts of the community, with stone and brick exteriors over framing that was never designed around today's plumbing loads. Add in private wells and septic systems that are common on the larger wooded parcels out here, and you get two failure points a city homeowner rarely thinks about: well pressure tank failures that flood utility rooms, and septic backups that complicate what would otherwise be a straightforward water cleanup. Mature tree cover across these lots also means root intrusion into older clay or cast iron drain lines, which shows up as slow backups long before anyone notices a leak.

Basements are the other recurring issue. We see this pattern most during the spring thaw and late-summer storm season, when the ground is already saturated and has nowhere left to send the water.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Garrison

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, since long gravel driveways and detached garages on some Garrison properties mean we can't always stage equipment right at the door. From there we inspect the structure with moisture meters and thermal imaging, because stone and masonry walls common in this area hold moisture differently than drywall does: the surface can read dry while the wall cavity behind it is still saturated. Drying is calibrated to that reality rather than a standard timeline, with daily monitoring until moisture content in the framing and subfloor returns to a stable baseline, dried in line with IICRC S500 drying standards.

If the source involves a well or septic system, we coordinate with your well or septic service provider before we close up any walls, since re-flooding is a real risk if the underlying mechanical issue isn't fixed first. ## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Garrison: a sump pump failure in a stone foundation home doesn't always announce itself the way it would in a newer house. Older stone and rubble foundations can wick moisture slowly through the mortar joints for days before it's visible on a finished basement wall, which means by the time you see a stain, the wood framing behind it has often already been wet long enough for mold to begin forming. If your sump pump has failed even briefly, it's worth having moisture levels checked even if nothing looks wrong yet.
