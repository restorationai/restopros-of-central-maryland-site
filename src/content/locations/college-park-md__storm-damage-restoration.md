College Park sits in Prince George's County along the Anacostia watershed, where Paint Branch and its tributaries run close to residential streets and the University of Maryland campus. Heavy summer thunderstorms and the occasional hurricane remnant moving up from the Chesapeake push these waterways past their banks fast, and low-lying yards near campus can pond for days after the rain stops. The city also carries a mature tree canopy, oaks and tulip poplars planted decades ago that line older residential blocks, and those same trees are the first thing to come down in a wind event or ice storm, often onto roofs, fences, and parked cars. Add in a housing stock that ranges from postwar bungalows near the university to newer construction further from Route 1, and you get two very different storm damage profiles in the same zip code: older roofs and siding that fail at lower wind speeds, and newer homes where the damage is more likely to be a single failure point, a flashing detail or a gutter system overwhelmed by volume.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in College Park

The first visit is about stopping further loss. We tarp open roof sections, board broken windows, and start water extraction if rain has gotten into the structure. From there we document everything with photos and moisture readings before any debris moves, because that record is what your adjuster will want to see. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and we track moisture in both the visible surface and the framing behind it. Older College Park homes with plaster or full-dimension lumber framing tend to hold moisture longer than newer drywall construction, so we extend monitoring on those properties rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule. Once the structure reads dry, we scope the repair work and coordinate next steps, whether that's roofing, drywall, or flooring replacement.
