Mold doesn't wait for a convenient season in Kensington. Between the humid Mid-Atlantic summers and the number of homes here with older masonry foundations and finished basements, a slow leak or a damp crawlspace can turn into a colonized wall cavity in less time than most homeowners expect. If you're smelling that musty, earthy odor near a baseboard or seeing dark speckling creep up a basement wall, the window to handle it as a simple cleanup rather than a full remediation job is shorter than you'd think.

## Why Kensington Properties See Mold Issues

Kensington sits in a part of Montgomery County where summer humidity regularly sits in the 60-80% range for weeks at a time, and that moisture load doesn't disappear when it gets dark. A lot of the housing stock in and around Kensington dates to the early-to-mid 20th century, with masonry and block foundations that were never built with a vapor barrier in mind. Add a finished basement, an older HVAC system that short-cycles instead of dehumidifying properly, or a gutter system that's fallen a season behind on maintenance, and you have the three ingredients mold needs: a moisture source, a stagnant air pocket, and an organic surface like drywall paper or wood framing to feed on. We also see it behind refrigerators and under kitchen sinks in homes where a slow supply line drip went unnoticed for months, which is a different problem than a flood but produces the same colonized drywall.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Kensington

We start with a visual and moisture-meter assessment to map out how far the problem has actually traveled, because mold on a wall surface rarely tells the whole story of what's happening inside the cavity. From there we isolate the work area with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't migrate into HVAC returns or adjacent rooms while we work, following the practices outlined in the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard. Affected porous materials like drywall, insulation, and in some cases subfloor get removed and bagged for disposal rather than treated in place, since those materials rarely dry back to a safe condition once colonized. Non-porous surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial solution, and HEPA air scrubbers run through the containment zone to bring airborne spore counts down before we close the job out.

## Kensington Insurance & HOA Coordination

A fair number of Kensington properties sit inside homeowners associations or small planned communities, and some of those associations have their own notification requirements before interior demolition work starts, especially in attached or semi-attached units where a shared wall is involved. We document moisture readings, photos, and the scope of removed materials throughout the job, which is the same documentation most insurance carriers want to see if the mold traces back to a covered water loss like a pipe failure or a roof leak. Mold itself is frequently excluded or capped under standard homeowners policies unless it's tied to a sudden, covered event, so we'll walk through what we're seeing on-site and flag early whether the underlying cause looks like something your carrier is likely to cover.

## Local note

Homes close to the Rock Creek watershed, including a good stretch of Kensington, tend to run with a slightly higher ambient basement humidity than properties on higher, better-drained ground elsewhere in the county, even when there's no visible leak. That baseline dampness means a dehumidifier that's merely "keeping up" in a Kensington basement might actually be losing ground slowly over a season, which is why we check relative humidity readings against outdoor dew point rather than relying on a single in-room number when we're deciding whether a space needs mechanical drying before remediation starts.
