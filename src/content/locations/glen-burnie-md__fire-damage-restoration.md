## Why Glen Burnie Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Glen Burnie's housing stock runs the gamut from post-war Cape Cods and brick ranchers near the older sections of the city to newer townhome developments closer to Arundel Mills. A lot of the mid-century homes were built with oil furnaces that are still in service, and a "puff-back," an oil burner misfire that blows soot through ductwork and into every room, is one of the most common fire-related calls we get from this part of Anne Arundel County. It doesn't look like a dramatic house fire, but the fine black residue coats walls, carpet, and HVAC components the same way a structural fire would, and it needs the same kind of professional cleanup. ## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Glen Burnie

Every fire loss starts with a walkthrough to separate what's salvageable from what isn't, because soot type changes the plan. Dry smoke from a fast, hot fire wipes off certain surfaces fairly cleanly, while wet smoke from a smoldering fire, the kind you get with upholstery or mattress fires, leaves a sticky residue that smears if you use the wrong technique. We board up broken windows and tarp damaged roofing to secure the structure, then run HEPA air scrubbers to pull soot particulate and odor compounds out of the air before we touch surfaces. Structural cleaning follows, working from ceiling down to floor so we're not re-contaminating areas we've already cleaned. In homes with central HVAC, we inspect and clean the ductwork separately, since soot circulated through a system during the fire keeps re-depositing on clean surfaces until it's addressed.
