## Why Germantown Properties See Water Damage Issues

A lot of Germantown's housing went up during the suburban build-out of the 1970s through the 1990s, which means basements with slab-on-grade or poured foundations, sump pumps that are now decades old, and PVC and copper supply lines that are reaching the age where pinhole leaks and fitting failures become common. Montgomery County's Piedmont clay soil holds water close to foundations after heavy rain, so grading issues that seemed minor for years can suddenly push groundwater against a basement wall during a wet spring. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Germantown

The first call is about stopping the source and getting water off the floor before it migrates into subfloor, baseboards, and wall cavities. Our technicians carry moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that's already traveled beyond what's visible, which matters in Germantown's finished basements where carpet pad and drywall can look dry on the surface while the material behind it stays saturated. Extraction equipment pulls standing water first, then we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and material mix, drying to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing at a timeline. In a split-level with a below-grade family room, that often means running equipment longer in the slab-adjacent areas than in the main living space above, since concrete and the soil beneath it release moisture more slowly than framed walls do.

## Germantown Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many of Germantown's townhome and condo communities have HOA bylaws that dictate who's responsible for pipe failures inside shared walls versus a homeowner's own unit, and getting that wrong can delay a claim by weeks.
