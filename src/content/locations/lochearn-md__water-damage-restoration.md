Lochearn's housing stock is mostly mid-century brick rowhomes and cape cods built from the 1940s through the 1960s, the kind of construction that was common across this part of Baltimore County as the city's population pushed outward after World War II. Those homes have cast iron or galvanized supply lines in many cases, finished basements added decades after the original build, and crawlspaces or partial basements that were never designed with modern sump systems in mind. When a water heater fails or a pipe splits behind a finished basement wall, the water has fewer places to go and more old materials to soak into than it would in newer construction.

## Why Lochearn Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of the calls we get from this area trace back to one of three sources: aging plumbing inside the home, grading and drainage around foundations that were never updated after decades of soil settling, and storm surges that overwhelm older storm drain infrastructure during heavy Mid-Atlantic downpours. Clay-heavy soil is common across much of Baltimore County, and it drains slowly. That means a heavy summer storm can leave water sitting against a foundation for hours longer than it would in sandier soil, pushing moisture through hairline cracks in block foundations that are typical in homes from this era. Basement finishing also complicates things, carpet pad, paneling, and drop ceilings installed over original concrete can trap water against structural materials long after the surface looks dry.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lochearn

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much access the property allows. From there we move to moisture mapping: checking subfloor, framing, and wall cavities with meters rather than guessing based on what's visible. In older Lochearn homes this step matters more than usual, because plaster and older drywall can look dry at the surface while the framing behind it is still saturated. We dry to the IICRC S500 standard, which means we're targeting actual moisture content readings in the material, not just a visual check or a set number of days. Drywall, baseboards, and flooring get removed only when it's necessary to reach trapped moisture, and we document everything with photos and readings for your insurance file as we go.

## Local note

One thing we watch for in this part of the county: a lot of Lochearn basements were finished with paneling glued directly to the foundation wall rather than furred out with a vapor gap. That glued paneling traps moisture against the block and accelerates efflorescence and mold growth behind the wall long before a stain shows up on the surface, so we pull a section early in the job just to check what's happening behind it rather than waiting for visible signs.
