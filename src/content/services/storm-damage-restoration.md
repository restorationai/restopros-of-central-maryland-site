**Storm damage emergency in Rockville?** We answer 24/7 and dispatch crews to secure the structure before wind-driven rain, a cracked roofline, or a fallen limb turns a single bad storm into weeks of secondary damage.

A downed branch through a roof deck, siding peeled back by straight-line winds, or water forcing its way under flashing after a derecho doesn't wait for business hours, and neither does the damage. Once the envelope of a home is breached, rain keeps finding new paths inward for as long as the opening sits exposed, soaking insulation, sheathing, and drywall that looked fine an hour earlier. The first priority after any severe weather event in Montgomery County is stopping that intrusion, then documenting and drying out what already got in.

## What Storm Damage Restoration actually involves

Storm response covers everything that happens between the moment a roof, window, or exterior wall is compromised and the point where the structure is dry, sealed, and ready for permanent repair. That usually means emergency tarping or board-up, extraction of any water that entered through the breach, and structural drying using the same air movers and dehumidifiers used on interior water losses, because wind-driven rain behaves like any other water intrusion once it's inside the wall or ceiling cavity. Crews also document wind speed indicators, impact points, and debris patterns on arrival, since this record becomes part of the insurance file. Depending on the damage, timelines run from a same-day tarp-and-dry to a multi-day process when saturated insulation or subfloor needs monitored drying before anyone can rebuild.

## Our process

1. **Emergency stabilization.** Tarping over roof breaches, board-up on broken windows or doors, and temporary bracing on compromised framing to stop further intrusion and secure the property.
2. **Debris and hazard clearing.** Removal of fallen limbs, loose shingles, and displaced material from the immediate work area so extraction and drying can begin safely.
3. **Water extraction.** Any rain or runoff that made it past the breach gets pulled from flooring, carpet pad, and standing pockets before it migrates further into wall cavities.
4. **Structural drying and moisture tracking.** Air movers, dehumidifiers, and moisture meters placed at the point of intrusion and monitored over several days, since storm water often travels further into framing and insulation than it appears at the surface.
5. **Scope handoff for repair.** Once moisture readings return to normal, we document the dried structure and provide a written scope so roofing, siding, or framing repairs can move forward with no guesswork about what's still wet.

## What separates a good storm response from a bad one

The most common mistake after severe weather is treating the visible damage as the whole problem. A tarp over a roof hole stops the rain but doesn't address the water that already tracked down into the attic insulation or ran along a truss into an interior wall. Crews that skip moisture mapping and go straight to cosmetic repair often seal in damp material, which sets up a mold problem weeks later that's far more expensive than the original storm claim. Adjusters reviewing a storm claim typically want photo documentation of the damage pattern (consistent with wind or impact, not pre-existing wear), moisture readings showing the extent of intrusion, and a clear timeline between the weather event and the mitigation response. Jobs that are well documented from the first site visit move through claims faster and with fewer disputes over what's covered.

Central Maryland sees two distinct storm windows: summer thunderstorm and hurricane remnants moving up from the Chesapeake between June and October, and winter nor'easters that bring high wind alongside heavy precipitation from December through March.
