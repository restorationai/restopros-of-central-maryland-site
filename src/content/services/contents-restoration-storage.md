When a pipe bursts or smoke rolls through a living room, the structure usually gets the headline, but it's the contents, furniture, electronics, clothing, photographs, keepsakes, that homeowners worry about most once the initial shock wears off. Soot settles into upholstery fibers within hours. Water wicks up table legs and swells particleboard cabinetry overnight. Left in place, those items can delay the structural drying or rebuild and become a secondary source of odor or mold. Contents restoration and storage separates what can be saved from what can't, documents it for your claim, and gets it out of the way so the rest of the job can move forward.

## What Contents Restoration & Storage actually involves

This service covers everything that isn't nailed to the house: furniture, rugs, clothing, electronics, documents, artwork, and personal items affected by water, fire, smoke, or biohazard exposure. The work typically starts with a room-by-room inventory and photo documentation before anything moves, which becomes the backbone of your insurance claim. From there, items are sorted into what can be cleaned on-site, what needs to go to a dedicated facility for deeper treatment, and what's unsalvageable. Techniques vary by material: ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods and small objects, specialized dry cleaning or ozone treatment for textiles and upholstery, and electronics or document recovery protocols that require more care than a standard wipe-down. Climate-controlled storage holds restorable contents while structural drying, demolition, or rebuild happens at the property, and a pack-back crew returns everything once the space is ready.

## Our process

1. **Inventory and documentation.** Every item is photographed, logged, and assigned a condition note before it leaves the property. This list becomes the Contents Claim Inventory your adjuster will reference.
2. **Sort and pack-out.** Items are separated by material type and contamination level, then boxed or crated for transport. Contaminated and clean items are never packed together.
3. **Cleaning and restoration.** Hard contents go through ultrasonic cleaning; textiles and upholstery are treated with appropriate dry cleaning, wet cleaning, or ozone methods depending on the soil type; electronics and documents follow separate recovery steps suited to their sensitivity.
4. **Climate-controlled storage.** Restorable items are held in a controlled environment, protected from the humidity swings that can undo cleaning work before the home is ready for them back.
5. **Pack-back.** Once structural repairs are complete, items are returned, unpacked, and placed according to the original inventory so the home comes back together the way it left it.

## What separates a good contents response from a bad one

The most common mistake is skipping the detailed inventory step to save time. Without item-level documentation and photos taken before anything is touched, disputes over what was damaged, what was pre-existing, and what the item was actually worth become harder to resolve with the carrier. A second mistake is storing wet or smoke-affected items alongside clean ones, which can cross-contaminate otherwise salvageable contents with odor or moisture. Electronics and documents also get mishandled more often than people expect: powering on a water-exposed device before it's been properly dried can cause damage that wasn't there to begin with, and paper documents need specific drying techniques rather than air drying, which causes warping and ink bleed. A careful operator treats the inventory as a legal document, keeps contaminated and clean items physically separated from pack-out through storage, and uses material-appropriate cleaning rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. Adjusters pay attention to whether the inventory matches the claim, whether cleaning methods are documented, and whether unsalvageable items are clearly justified rather than assumed.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Rockville's humid summers make storage conditions matter more than most homeowners expect. Items sitting in a non-climate-controlled trailer or garage during a July heat wave can mildew even if they tested dry at pack-out. Winter brings the opposite risk: homes built before the 1980s around Montgomery County often have older HVAC and plumbing that fail during freeze events, sending contents crews out to pack rooms while pipes are still being repaired. Either way, proper climate-controlled storage protects the restoration work already done on an item from being undone by the next season's weather.
