## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

A burst pipe is a different animal from a slow leak. You're usually dealing with pressurized water that's been spraying, pooling, or running for an unknown stretch of time before anyone noticed, often behind a wall, under a slab, or inside a ceiling cavity. The work starts with locating and isolating the water source (not just shutting off the main, but finding where the pipe actually failed), then moving fast into extraction with truck-mounted or portable pumps and wet vacs to pull standing water before it migrates further into subfloor, baseboards, and adjoining rooms. From there it's structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers set to pull moisture out of framing, drywall, and flooring systems, with moisture readings logged over several days to confirm materials are actually dry, not just dry to the touch. Depending on how the pipe failed and where, some drywall, insulation, or flooring may need to come out entirely rather than dry in place, especially around older copper or galvanized lines common in Rockville's mid-century housing stock.

## Our process

1. **Locate and stop the source.** We find exactly where the pipe failed, shut down the correct supply line (not just guess at the main), and confirm no residual pressure is still feeding the leak.
2. **Extract standing water.** Pumps and extraction equipment remove bulk water from floors, carpet, and any cavities before we touch drying equipment, since drying wet carpet pad under standing water wastes time and electricity.
3. **Open up and inspect affected cavities.** Baseboards, drywall, and flooring near the break get checked and, where needed, opened so trapped water in wall cavities or under flooring doesn't sit hidden and keep feeding mold growth.
4. **Set structural drying equipment and monitor.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in per IICRC S500 drying guidelines, and we track moisture content in framing and subfloor daily until readings confirm the structure is dry, not just the surface.
5. **Coordinate repair.** Once drying is confirmed, we scope out what needs to be put back: drywall, flooring, trim, and we document the full loss for your insurance claim.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake on a pipe break is drying the visible surface and calling it done while water is still sitting inside a wall cavity or under a hardwood floor. Water behind plaster or inside a joist bay can read dry on a surface moisture meter while the material underneath stays saturated for days, and that's exactly the condition mold needs to start colonizing, often within 24 to 48 hours in a warm, humid house. A thorough operator pulls baseboards, checks wall cavities with a moisture meter or thermal imaging, and documents readings at the start and throughout drying, not just a single reading at the end. Insurance adjusters look for exactly that kind of documentation: dated moisture logs, photos of the affected cavities before and after materials are removed, and a clear record of when the source was stopped. Skipping any of that step can mean a claim gets questioned later, or worse, a wall gets closed back up over material that was never actually dry.

Central Maryland sees its highest volume of burst pipe calls during hard freezes in January and February, when uninsulated pipes in crawlspaces, exterior walls, and unheated additions are most at risk, along with a second wave each spring when freeze-thaw cycles stress fittings that were already weakened.
