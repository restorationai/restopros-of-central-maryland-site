A sewer line backup or septic overflow is not a cleanup job you want to handle with a mop and a bucket of bleach: the water carries bacteria, viruses, and waste solids that can contaminate flooring, drywall, and anything porous within hours.

## What Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization actually involves

Sewage intrusion is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated tier recognized in the restoration industry, whether it comes from a backed-up sewer line, a failed septic tank, an overwhelmed sump pump, or a toilet that overflows during a storm. That classification changes everything about how the job gets handled compared to a clean-water leak. Porous materials that soak up contaminated water, carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, often can't be saved and have to come out rather than just dried. The work involves pumping or extracting standing wastewater, removing and bagging contaminated debris for proper disposal, cleaning and disinfecting hard surfaces with hospital-grade antimicrobials, and drying the structure while monitoring for lingering moisture inside wall cavities and subfloor. Depending on how far the water traveled and how long it sat before the crew arrived, a single backed-up bathroom can turn into a multi-room job if wastewater tracked under flooring or down a hallway.

## Our process

1. **PPE staging and contamination assessment.** Crews suit up in protective gear before entering the affected area and identify the source (sewer line, septic, plumbing backup) and the extent of contact with flooring, walls, and contents.
2. **Extraction of standing wastewater.** Pumps and wet vacuums remove raw sewage and contaminated water first, before any cleaning begins, to limit how far contamination spreads.
3. **Removal of unsalvageable porous materials.** Carpet, pad, baseboards, and drywall that absorbed Category 3 water are cut out and bagged for disposal rather than cleaned in place.
4. **Cleaning and sanitization of remaining surfaces.** Hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial solution to address bacteria and odor-causing residue.
5. **Drying, monitoring, and odor control.** Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the space to a dry standard while moisture readings confirm the structure underneath is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.

## What separates a good sewage cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake on a sewage job is treating it like a regular water loss: cleaning the carpet instead of removing it, or drying drywall that should have been cut out. Category 3 water that gets dried in place instead of removed can leave bacteria and odor behind even after the surface looks clean. A thorough response documents the contamination source and affected materials with photos before anything is removed, which matters when an adjuster reviews the claim later. It also means tracking exactly which materials were disposed of and where, since sewage debris has to be bagged and disposed of properly, not just hauled to a regular dumpster. Crews that skip moisture mapping under flooring or behind baseboards often miss pockets of contaminated water that surface as odor or mold weeks later.
